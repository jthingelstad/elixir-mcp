# Write how your clan runs

Every clan runs differently, so Elixir Clan has no rules of its own. The
clan's **policy** says what it counts, how Elder works, how long a quiet
member has, and which [actions](/docs/clan-actions) to raise. Until a
leader saves one, nothing is judged: no standing, no actions, no
awards.

## Who writes it

The leader and co-leaders, under **Manage ▸ Policy**, once their player
is [verified](/docs/verify). Leaders can prepare and save a policy at any
clan size; the small-clan roster page offers leaders **Prepare policy** alongside
**Open Recruit**. Operational reviews, Actions, standing and awards remain paused
below 10 members and resume at 10. Saving below ten creates no announcement
Action. Policy is Elixir's agreement for the clan, not a game setting.
Every member reads an active policy on
[Standing](/docs/standing), under **How it works here**.

## Starting points

The **About** tab starts from what the clan is for. Pick one and every
tab is filled from it, yours to change before you save:

| Starting point | Counts | How strict |
|---|---|---|
| A war clan | Clan Wars and donations | strict |
| A climbing clan | ranked play and trophy road | standard |
| A donation clan | donations | standard |
| A social clan | nothing; Elders by hand | relaxed |
| A bit of everything | all four | standard |

**How strict** (relaxed, standard or strict) sets where the minimums
and clocks start. **Playing together** can be said but is never
measured: the game does not say who plays with whom. On a saved policy,
a starting point replaces the whole draft. Every preset turns on the
inactivity clock, welcomes, departure questions and announcements; review
those tabs before saving. Changing How strict alone does not retune fields already
in the draft.

**Clan War participation** comes first on About and in the **Draft agreement**
summary, and the **Clan Wars** tab links back to it. It records the leaders'
intent: participating, not participating, or not specified, which is where a
policy starts. It is separate from **Count Clan Wars**, which controls scoring
and minimums; starting points and category switches never set or change it.

The Discord agent's scheduled **war-deck check** uses this saved intent
([Agent context](#agent-context)). Participating permits the check only in the
appropriate war phase after a fresh policy read succeeds. Not participating
suppresses the check; not specified, unavailable or stale context defers it.
The agent refreshes before planning and immediately before firing, and the
final read must be no more than 60 seconds old. This gate applies to the
scheduled war-deck check; other agent content keeps its own rules.

## The tabs

| Tab | What it decides |
|---|---|
| About | what the clan is for, and how strict |
| Clan Wars, Ranked play, Donations, Trophy road | whether each is counted, its window (trophy road has none) and its minimum |
| Elder | by hand or by participation, each category's weight, days in the clan before a promotion, the band, how many reviews a promotion or demotion needs and how long a declined one waits, what members see, and the window for checking a completed action |
| Minimums | the shared window and any-one/all rule for enabled thresholds above zero; these affect Elder eligibility and inactivity grace, separately from category score windows |
| Inactivity | **Suggest removals**: the getting quiet, at risk and removal days, grace for meeting the minimums, whether Elders are shown as Protected, how long members may mark themselves away, and whether to ask quiet members ([the inactivity clock](#the-inactivity-clock)) |
| Arrivals and departures | **Ask about departures** and **Suggest welcoming newcomers** |
| Announcements | **Announce each season's awards** and **Tell the clan when the rules change** |

A tab that is off shows only its switch; turning it on fills it from the
clan's How strict. Every setting carries a line saying what it does.
[Standing](/docs/standing) explains what the categories, minimums and
band mean.

### The inactivity clock

A member's clock counts the days since their last battle in Elixir's
record, or since they joined if they have not battled since. A rise in
the battle count on their profile counts as play too. **Getting quiet**,
**At risk** and **Removal action at** are each a number of days on that
one clock: with at risk at 7 and the removal action at 10, a member is at
risk after 7 days without a battle and the leaders get a removal Action
on day 10, not day 17. The removal day is never before at risk. Grace for
meeting the minimums adds days to the removal day. A hold, or a member's
own away, keeps the Action from being raised, and Elders are Protected
unless the policy includes them.

A member who reaches at risk is asked whether they are away, when the
policy asks quiet members. If Elixir could not read a member's activity,
or the player has left, nothing is raised for them until the next run
can read it. Removing anyone stays a leader's call in the game.

## What this policy can do today

Once a policy is saved, the top of the page lists each judgment it asks
for, with one of:

| Status | Means |
|---|---|
| Live | Elixir Clan acts on it now |
| Needs more evidence | it will act once the record has what is named: 10 members, a saved policy, or one finished review week |
| Off | the policy does not ask for it |

The judgments listed are promoting and demoting Elders, removing
inactive members, asking quiet members if they are away, asking how a
member left, and welcoming newcomers.

## Preview before you save

**Preview the last reviews** reads the clan's record and shows what the
last four reviews would have said under the draft, beside the current
policy: the Elder band before and after, and each member who would read
differently. No policy, Action, award or decision is saved or raised by a
preview, and no model is called. The member-count observation and normal
recorded-read audit are updated; the Preview result itself is not saved.
Holds and decision cooldowns are not applied in this comparison, so it is
not an exact forecast of pending Actions. Editing any setting clears it.

## Saving

Saving makes a new version; the first is version 1. Add a line on why,
if you like; the versions list keeps who saved each and when. A setting
out of range is refused, with the tab that holds it opened and the
field marked. The clan is read afresh under the new version, which can
raise or withdraw pending Actions at the next evaluation, including reviews
of recorded weeks. Completed decisions and saved award grants stay as
recorded; awards have a separate setup. A stale browser version is refused
instead of overwriting a newer policy, and unsaved edits survive a refetch.
No policy save changes game settings, kicks a member or sends a message.

Saving does not restart review streaks or the activity clock. Recorded review
boundaries are replayed under the new values; the current Trophy Road figure
is used when that category counts. A pending recommendation that still
qualifies retains the evidence and policy version saved when it was raised.
Current thresholds, grace and waiting periods apply at reevaluation without
rewriting previous decisions.

When **Tell the clan when the rules change** is on, a save raises
**Tell the clan how it runs** for the leaders: chat copy with an explicit durable Inbox alternative,
saying how the clan runs the first time, and what changed after.

## The leaders pages

Under **Manage**, beside the policy:

- **Board**: every member as the policy reads them today, grouped as
  actionable, building, held or clear. Group links jump to the members;
  held promotion and demotion counts are separate from removal evidence.
  The inactivity clock shows here as triage for every member
  ([the inactivity clock](#the-inactivity-clock)).
  Open a member for their evidence,
  a note, and a **hold**, which shields removal eligibility for a member
  who said they will be away, until a date or until cleared.
- **History**: the joins, leaves and role changes the record saw, with
  how each leave was answered; every action the clan has had, with its
  outcome and who decided it; and the holds.
- **Scout**: paste an applicant's tag and see their numbers; with a
  policy, also whether they would meet its minimums today. Elders see it
  too, and it works before a policy.

Awards have pages of their own that every member sees, **Award races** and
**Award history**; the leaders set the awards up and grant leaders' picks on
Award races ([Awards and trophies](/docs/clan-awards)).

## Clan settings

**Manage ▸ Settings** holds what belongs to the whole clan, with or
without a policy:

- **What Elixir Clan records in Elixir**: the kinds of facts shared
  with Elixir, when each is shared and who sees it there. Kicks and
  leaves, promotions and demotions, awards, messages to the clan and
  where the award races stand go to everyone verified in the clan; kicks,
  leaves and the award races also to the clan's agent; a member's away
  only to the leaders.
- **Social**: the clan map, where members add a city or region and see
  each other's local time. Only the clan's verified members see it, and
  nothing of it goes to Elixir. Only the leader and co-leaders can change
  whether the clan's social features are on; other members opening Settings
  directly can read that status without a switch. The clan's activity
  channel is Social's own page in the rail, not a setting here ([The
  clan's activity in Discord](#the-clans-activity-in-discord)).
- **Actions in Discord**: a webhook from one of the clan's Discord channels.
  Each open action leaders or elders can take is posted there and
  marked when it closes ([Actions in the clan's
  Discord](/docs/clan-actions#actions-in-the-clans-discord)). Connecting
  it posts a short message to check it; the address is kept encrypted
  and never shown again. Only the leader and co-leaders can connect,
  replace or remove it.
- **The clan's own model**: an Anthropic key of the clan's own, which
  lets a model draft the recruiting pitch in Recruit and, on an open
  action, the reviewed message (clan chat or a Clan Leader Message) for a promotion, demotion, awards
  announcement or policy-change announcement, plus welcome and post-decision
  removal chat lines, plus a departure message after a leader confirms
  Kicked or Left. Saved recruiting words provide the clan's voice. The
  model is told what Elixir records about the clan, the Action and the
  member it is about, by name ([what it
  receives](/docs/clan-actions#the-words-to-send)), with what the leader
  asks for in **What should it say?** as written; tags, notes saved on
  an Action (anyone's), holds, a removal's rationale or inactivity
  numbers and a member's share of fame are never sent. Chat
  drafts use fixed tone choices for welcomes, removals and farewells.
  Everything it writes is a draft a leader edits and copies into the
  game, marked where it carries a number or a name it was not given. It
  never makes or completes a decision.
  Elixir Clan chooses the model for each use, and Settings shows which:
  Claude Opus 5.5 drafts the recruiting pitch and the messages on
  Actions, and Claude Sonnet 5.5 edits the activity channel.
  When the key cannot reach that model, the use takes the other of the
  two, then Claude Haiku 5.5; it never falls back to an older model, and
  a key that reaches none of the three is not accepted. Which models the
  key reaches is checked with Anthropic when the key is added and again
  about once a day when a leader opens Settings. The key and its monthly
  cap are the leaders' choice; the model is not.
  Settings shows each use's tokens and about what it cost, and the
  month's total: the tokens at Anthropic's list prices (the date is on
  the page), an estimate rather than the bill. The leader or a co-leader
  may set a monthly cap in dollars. Once the month's (UTC) estimate
  reaches it, the model drafts nothing more until a leader raises or
  removes the cap or the month turns; a draft that starts under the cap
  may finish a little over it. The cap stays when the key is replaced.
  It is Elixir Clan's own stop: a spend limit on the Anthropic account
  is the one Anthropic keeps.
  The one use nobody reviews is the activity channel's editor, when a
  leader turns it on (below); its runs are counted apart, and the cap
  stops them too. A use whose answer never arrived (the provider may
  still have charged for it) is counted toward the cap at its estimate.

## The clan's activity in Discord

**Social ▸ Discord**, a page in the rail for the leader and co-leaders,
with or without a policy, is where they
connect a Discord webhook of its own: not the one Actions go to, and not
a person's [timeline cross-post](/docs/timeline). From then on the
channel tells what happens in the clan, as its members read it. It is an
editor, not a copy of the timeline: Elixir gathers what one moment
produced into one story and writes it as a sentence or two, with no
time and no link, for example "Ada moved up to Magic Academy with a 3-0
win over keyluis and set a new best of 12,005 trophies, joining Bo
there. Their Hog Rider cycle deck is 9-2 on ladder this season." A story
told more than three hours late says when in words ("Yesterday").

- **What makes a story**: one member's one battle (the arena it moved
  them into, the new best it set, the league it promoted them to, all in
  one post); the same kind of milestone reached by several members at
  once (up to five, one post naming each); a standout sitting, one post
  that grows; and anything else on its own. From the record Elixir adds
  what it knows: the deck the battle was won with and its record in that
  mode this season (ladder or Ranked), a second promotion in a day, the
  clanmates already in the arena a member reached (also when the roster
  saw the move before its battle arrived), and for a sitting its modes,
  its best win and its run.
- **One sitting, one post a day**: a member's sitting (battles less
  than half an hour apart) is told once a day. A new story from a battle
  or a sitting the channel already posted about that member today, and
  from before that post went up, is part of it: the post is edited to
  tell it too when it is under two hours old and one of the channel's
  three newest, otherwise the story is skipped. A member's second
  sitting in a day is skipped the same way. A promotion or a new best
  from a later battle is still its own post.
- **Skipped, with the reason**: a badge or mastery level-up below its
  top level (the top level and a new badge are told); a story already
  posted that only grew (a sitting with more battles but the same best
  win and its run not five longer); and something already told (the same
  member's same battle, or the same arena, league, trophy rung or
  milestone reached again within a week); and what is already told
  today (the same sitting as an earlier post about the member). Each
  skip is listed on the page with its reason.
- **Held a few minutes**: a story is posted once nothing has been added
  to it for four minutes, or ten minutes after it began, so what one
  battle produced arrives as one post: usually about six minutes after
  the activity lands in Elixir, at most about a quarter of an hour. A
  post is edited in place only when its story truly grows (a sitting's
  new best win, its run five longer), never posted twice.

- **What is posted**: three groups a leader switches. **Members**: who
  joins and who departs, and role changes made in the game. **Milestones**:
  members' standout sessions, badges, arenas, ranked promotions, new
  bests, collection levels, career wins and card unlocks, and the clan's
  awards. **Clan Wars**: the week's bracket, the boat crossing the finish
  line and how the week finished.
- **The policy decides the defaults**: Members and Milestones are on;
  Clan Wars is on when the policy says the clan takes part, and off until
  a leader turns it on when the policy does not say. When the policy says
  the clan does not take part, Clan Wars cannot be turned on. A leader's
  switch holds until a leader sets it back to the policy's default.
  Without a policy nothing is posted.
- **A departure** is posted as the roster sees it: the member departed.
  When a leader says which it was on the departure's action, that same
  message is edited to say the member was removed, or left on their own;
  it is never a second post, and a kick is never told as a leave.
- **When**: posting starts when the webhook is connected (or turned back
  on); nothing earlier is posted. Each story goes out a few minutes after
  the activity lands in Elixir, never on a schedule. Nothing pings
  anyone; items only the leaders may see are never posted.
- **Edited by the clan's own model**: with the clan's own model key
  set, a leader can have the clan's model edit the channel in the clan's
  voice, described in up to 400 characters. Its posts are made without
  anyone reading them first. The model is handed a run's stories, up to
  12, each with Elixir's own words for it, and the clan (its name,
  recruiting words, goals, member count and last war weeks), the
  moment's own facts (the battle and who it beat, the badge, the best
  set, the week's place) and, for a member, what Elixir records about
  them: trophies, best, arena, career wins, collection level, time in
  the clan and any earlier stint, recent war weeks as decks and points,
  the clan's awards they hold as announced this season and last, their
  last three moments from the past two weeks while the channel posts
  Milestones (never the one being posted; for up to eight members in
  one run), how often the channel already told about them this week,
  and the channel's own words in its last three posts about them
  today. A departure's story is told only the member's time in the clan
  and the weeks they played. Never a tag, a member's share of fame, an
  award not yet announced, or anything private to Clan.
  Before it answers, the model may look things up in the clan's record,
  read-only, in up to two rounds of at most six lookups each, a member
  found by name (a name two current members share is not looked up): a
  current member's record as above; a current member's battles in the last
  seven days (up to ten: the mode, the result and crowns, the opponent,
  the deck's archetype, the trophy change and roughly when, in words);
  and what the channel posted in the last three days. Then it edits: it
  may tell several stories in one post, skip a story with a short reason
  (shown on the page), and tells the rest in one to three sentences. A
  departure is never skipped and never told with another story, and
  neither is a post being edited in place.
  A departure keeps Elixir's sentence for it word for word (departed,
  was removed, left on their own); the model may add only a thank-you
  or a farewell in English, never a reason or anything else.
  A post that drops or changes a member's or opponent's name, carries a
  number or a name it was neither given nor looked up, says a time of
  day, leaves out when a story over three hours late happened ("earlier
  today", "yesterday"), changes a departure's sentence or adds more than a thank-you or a farewell to it, tells a
  story another post already told, carries a link or a mention, or runs
  past 600 characters is refused, and its stories are Elixir's own. So is every
  story the model leaves out, and every story when the model cannot be
  used or has not answered in time. At most 50 editor runs a day (a run
  is one to three requests: its lookups, then its answer), counted
  apart from the leaders' drafts and shown in the key's use log with
  each request; the clan's monthly cap, when set, stops them as it stops
  drafts. What a run costs is on [Limits](/docs/limits).
  **Preview the last 5 in this voice** hands the five newest stories
  the channel could post from the last week to the editor, in the voice
  in the box (saved or not), and shows what it would do: each post with
  the stories it tells and Elixir's versions of them, each skip with its
  reason, and each story kept in Elixir's words with why when the model's
  post or skip was refused. It is one request with no lookups, so a
  real run may say more. It posts nothing and counts as one of the
  leaders' drafts for the day.
  Without a key, the page points to **The clan's own model** in Settings.
- Connecting posts a short hello to check the webhook; it is kept
  encrypted and shown only shortened. A webhook may end in `?thread_id=`
  and a thread's id, to post into one thread; a forum channel's webhook
  is refused, since a forum takes threads, not messages. If Discord stops
  taking it, posting turns off and the page says why. If Discord refuses
  one post, only that post is skipped and the page shows what Discord
  said. Posts go at most about 25 a minute, under Discord's limit for a
  channel. Pausing or disconnecting leaves the messages already posted.
- The page lists the channel's last 40 deliveries and skips from the
  past 30 days, each post whole as it was posted, with any the clan's
  model wrote marked **by the clan's model**, and each skip with what it would
  have said and why it was skipped, by Elixir's rule or by the clan's
  model. It also counts the posts not
  delivered; a skip is not one of them.
- A leader or co-leader can rate each post that reached the channel with
  👍 or 👎, and a note if they like. Elixir cannot see the clan's channel,
  so this is how it learns which posts read well there: the rating goes
  to Elixir as feedback about that post from the leader's own account,
  and each leader sees only their own.

## Agent context

An agent may read its assigned clan’s explicit Clan War intent only after a
separate, approved private-context grant. `clans_context` takes no clan argument
and returns `context` with exactly eight fields: `schema_version`, `clan_tag`,
`status`, `reason`, `war_intent`, `policy_version`, `policy_saved_at`, and `read_at`.
The MCP envelope also carries the usual applied, notes, docs and meta fields.
There are no scoring values, leader notes, player names, member decisions or other
private policy details. Standard game scopes alone grant no policy access.

Each grant binds one agent, its actual owner, its assigned clan and one existing
service credential. Every read checks the current owner and assignment, an
approved person owner’s verified primary/alt claim, and open recorded membership
in that clan. Friends, watched players and a remembered former clan cannot qualify.
Ownership or assignment changes durably revoke the grant; explicit revocation,
credential revocation, suspended accounts, lost verification or closed membership
refuse the read. Permission does not transfer to another clan or owner.

A successful read with no saved policy returns unknown/no_policy with null
revision/save time; missing or unsupported intent returns
unknown/war_intent_unspecified. Unknown never means not participating. Saved
revision numbers are local to one clan; saved_at is provenance, while read_at
times this current read. An old saved policy can still be current. Refresh at
startup, before planning and immediately before firing an affected routine.
Defer that routine on unknown, a failed refresh, unsupported schema or wrong clan;
never substitute a cached decision.

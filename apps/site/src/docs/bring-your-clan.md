# Bring your clan

[Clan](/clan) is the part of Elixir for being in a clan: the
roster as the record sees it, a weekly report, and, once the leaders
have written how the clan runs, standing, actions and awards. It never
acts in the game and never decides for anyone; the leaders do. Every
page is behind sign-in, and only people sign in. Today an agent gets
one narrow read of the clan's war intent with an explicit grant
([Agent context](/docs/clan-agent-context#agent-context)). What you connect to
Elixir as yourself can read your own view here (the policy, your own
standing line and the Actions waiting for you), never anyone else's
([Your own view](/docs/clan-your-own-view#your-own-view)).

Clan is part of Elixir's shared application, account and storage. Your Elixir
sign-in opens Clan directly; there is no second consent or Clan account.

## Before you sign in

You need two things:

1. **An Elixir account.** Elixir is the account; there is no separate
   sign-up for Clan. [Your account](/docs/your-account) has how to create
   one by verifying your email.
2. **Your player on it.** Your primary player or an alt, under
   **Settings › Players & tracking**. A friend or a player you watch does not
   count: Clan goes by the players that are you.

Then open [/clan](/clan). Your existing Elixir session opens it directly;
a signed-out visit returns there after the common sign-in.

## Bring your clanmates

Once your primary player's clan is on record, Ladder's Season tab has
**Invite clanmates**, folded and named for the clan:

- **A line for clan chat**, within the game's 200 characters and written
  around what its chat filter masks. It ends with Elixir's address,
  elixir.poapkings.com, so the sentence before it still reads if the
  game ever hides the address.
- **A longer note for Discord or a message**, ending with the signup
  link, and your device's share sheet where it has one.

A clanmate who signs up and adds their player gets their own battle
history kept from then on and, because Elixir follows a new member's
clan for them ([Your player's clan](/docs/recording#your-players-clan)),
the clan's week in Monday's email. Elixir is free.

The link is Elixir's plain signup address, the same for everyone: no
invite code, nothing in it that says who sent it, and nothing records
who copied or shared a line. Inside Clan, a clan with a policy
also has **Spread the word** with the clan's own lines.

## Your role here

Clan reads your player's clan and role from the record. Your role here
is your role in the game, leader, co-leader, elder or member, **once
your player is [verified](/docs/verify)**. Until then you are a member
who reads, whatever your role in the game: you see what members see,
and nothing is done in your player's name, so no away notice, no
comments, and no place on the clan map. Verifying is one battle with a
deck Elixir names, usually under a minute; sign in to Clan again
afterwards.

A leader, co-leader or elder signing in unverified is asked to verify
first, or to go on as a member for now.

The record learns a role change when it next reads the clan's roster
from the game. So before the leader or a co-leader changes anything
(saving the policy, deciding an Action, a hold, a note, the clan's
settings), Clan checks that the roster was read within the last hour.
If it was not, Elixir reads it now and Clan says **Confirming your role
in the game; try again in a minute**, with **Try again**; nothing is
changed until then. A leader who was kicked or demoted in the game has
lost those powers by the time it lets them through. Reading pages, a
member's own away notice, the policy preview and Scout never wait.

If two of your players are in the same clan, the higher role counts.

**One clan at a time, by design.** Each clan in Clan has its own policy,
standing, Actions and awards, and nothing is pooled across clans. If
your players are in different clans (a clan family's main and its
feeders, say), Clan asks which one to work in and remembers it; switch
at any time from the **Clan** picker. A family is how Elixir *records* clans:
the several every-member clan slots Family limits give ([Roles](/docs/roles)).
Clan does not know which clans make up a family, only which clans your
own players are in.

## Finding your way

The head of every Clan page is the clan you are in: its name, your role
there and, while your player there is not verified, **Verify**. If your
players are in more than one clan, a **Clan** picker sits beside the name:
each clan with your role there, and **All your clans…** for the full list.
Picking a clan keeps you on the page you were on. With one clan there is
nothing to pick.

Below it, the tabs:

- **Feed**: what is waiting for you first, then the clan's last seven days,
  newest first: joins, departures and role changes, members' moments, and the
  highlights its members post, each with its battle. Your own numbers and the
  week so far sit beside it. The Feed shows you what Elixir's timeline would
  show you about this clan, no more: a member reads the clan's facts, and the
  leader and co-leaders also the ones that are theirs.
- **Standings**: each season's races and its winners, and your own trophy
  case ([Awards and trophies](/docs/clan-awards)).
- **War** ([War and the week](/docs/clan-season)): the running week and
  season, and each closed [week](/docs/clan-week) and season.
- **Members**: **Roster**; [Standing](/docs/standing), once the clan has a
  policy and 10 members; **Review** for the leader and co-leaders; **Map**,
  where verified members add a city or region and see each other's local
  time; **Recruit**, editable copy in one selected format, a personal note or
  a recruiting post; and **Scout** for elders and up. A name opens that
  player's [page](/docs/player-page), and its **Open in Clan** opens their
  page in this clan; your own has **Away** beside it when the clan lets
  members mark themselves away.
- **Policy**: [the policy](/docs/clan-policy) in every member's words: what
  the clan counts, its minimums, how Elder is earned and the removal clock,
  with Elder's weights, the version and who saved it. Anyone in the
  [clan directory](/docs/clan-directory) reads the same.
- **Run**, for the leader and co-leaders only, once verified: **Waiting** and
  **Decided**, the record of every decision ([Actions](/docs/clan-actions));
  the policy, section by section; then **Awards setup**, **Discord**,
  **Model & voice** and **Clan settings**. Anyone else who opens Run's
  address is told it is the leaders', and Elixir refuses its pages to them.

**Send feedback**, at the foot of the page, and **Report this** beside an
action, Standing and the award races both file into Elixir's one feedback
record, about Clan and the thing on screen, and the answer comes to your
[feedback](/docs/your-account#feedback) and your inbox.

On a phone the tabs stay in one row under the clan's name, scrolling
sideways when they do not all fit, with a count on the Feed when something is
waiting for you, and Run opens as its list of sections.

Coming to a clan (its address, [/clan](/clan) or coming back from sign-in)
opens the **Feed**, for every member and whether or not something is waiting.
With several clans and none chosen yet, [/clan](/clan) asks which. An address
that names a page always opens that page, and an older link to a clan page,
from mail or Discord, still opens the page that holds the thing.

## When the clan is not on Elixir

Clan works from what Elixir records. Elixir follows your primary player's
clan for you at activity scope once your player's profile shows it, when
you have a clan slot free ([Your player's
clan](/docs/recording#your-players-clan)). If your clan is not recorded
yet, Clan says so and sends you to **Settings › Players & tracking** to track the
clan; the roster arrives with Elixir's first read of it. A clan recorded with
its members' battle logs ([the clan and every member](/docs/recording)) gives
fuller battle numbers than one recorded at activity scope, and Clan
holds back a judgment the record cannot support rather than guess.

When profile or roster readings cannot establish your membership, Clan
says **Waiting for your clan record** and links to Players & tracking. A missing clan tag
or roster role does not prove you are outside a clan.

Only explicit absence in the latest admitted profiles for all your own players
shows **No clan in your recorded profiles**, dated by those readings. This
is not a live check: if it is still accurate, join a clan in the game;
otherwise wait for a fresh profile and clan roster and check again. Friends
and watched players do not decide your membership or grant roles.

## Ten members

Clan management starts at **10 members**. Below that, like Clan Wars,
leaders can prepare and save Policy; actions, standing and awards wait. Clan shows the roster
and every member's numbers, Recruit works for everyone and Scout for
elders and up. A Member who opens Scout’s address sees the role requirement
before any live read can be requested. A policy saved before the clan dropped below 10 is kept
and picks up again at 10.

## Before the leaders write a policy

Nothing is judged until the clan's leader or a co-leader writes the
clan's [policy](/docs/clan-policy): no actions, no standing, no
inactivity clock, no awards. Every member can still read the roster,
their own page and [Season](/docs/clan-season).
Members are shown a line to send the leaders, for clan chat or a
message, and the leaders are shown where to start.

Your own page labels weekly battle counts as recorded battles. Zero recorded
battles does not prove inactivity. Weeks before roster recording began are
marked; their counts do not establish membership or complete reading.

## Reads and access

Each page follows your role. Management controls stay with the leader and
co-leaders; an Elder can grant only the leaders' picks that allow Elders. A denied read explains the access requirement rather than
waiting indefinitely. An unavailable read says it could not be read, and does
not claim an empty record or permit a decision.

## Signing out

Signing out ends your Elixir browser session, in Clan and across Elixir.
The clan's policy, actions and awards stay as they are.

# Accounts and privacy

*Ratified by Jamie, 2026-10-12, in the sitting that drafted it.*

## What it is for

An account is how a person tells Elixir who they are in the game and
what to keep for them: their players, the relationships between those
players and the people they follow, where their mail goes, and the
agents and apps that act as them. One account is one person; everything
in Elixir (the console, Ladder, Clan, mail, a connected agent) acts as
that same person. [Jamie, 2026-09-03; your-account]

## Who it serves

- **Anyone who likes the game.** Signup is free and public: verify an
  email and the account opens. No privileged tier, collector token or
  extra capacity comes with it; a denied or disabled account never
  reopens through signup. There is no paid tier, and none is planned
  without Supercell's express approval; money is voluntary sponsorship
  that buys nothing. [Jamie, 2026-09-19, 2026-10-06]
- **The person's agents and apps.** Three kinds of principal: users,
  agents and integrations. An agent is the person's own, capped at its
  owner's tier, tracking in the person's pooled slots, never deleted
  (suspended or revoked). [Jamie, 2026-09-08, 2026-09-23, 2026-10-10]
- **The family's apps.** Sign in with Elixir carries the person to Drop
  and to a clan's tooling; the address goes only to a family app the
  person allows. [Jamie, 2026-09-25]

## Principles that decide the details

1. **Email is the identity, and Discord may become a way in.** No
   password; a code or link signs in; a sign-in lasts thirty days from
   last use and ninety at most. Elixir has many Discord features, so a
   Discord sign-in "may make a ton of sense" later; it would sign in to
   the same email-identified account, never a second identity. [Jamie,
   2026-10-12]
2. **A claim is trust; Verify is proof.** Adding a player says "this is
   me" and is taken on trust for everything a person does for
   themselves. Acting for a clan needs proof: one battle with a deck
   Elixir names, and a tag has one verified owner. [Jamie, 2026-09-12,
   2026-09-13]
3. **Roles never gate visibility, and a tier is earned in the game.**
   Tiers differ only in what Elixir records for you and how many calls
   you get; every account reads the whole record. [Jamie, 2026-09-05,
   2026-09-08] Each tier has an obvious unlock tied to game activity:
   **Member** is signup; **Leader** comes with being a verified leader
   or co-leader of a clan you track; **Family** comes with running a
   collector for Elixir. **Partner**, full access to Elixir's API
   surface, stays granted by Jamie for now, and "may not even be a
   thing": if it confuses the page, it is removed and the ladder is
   Member, Leader, Family. [Jamie, 2026-10-12]
4. **Four privacy buckets, settled.** Game data is public and recorded;
   a Clash Royale tag is public game data, not PII. Account data
   (claims, nicknames, connections, usage) is the account's. The email
   address is PII and goes only to family apps the person allows.
   Measurement is aggregate: never a per-recipient open or click, never
   a referral id. [Jamie, 2026-09-08, 2026-09-19, 2026-10-08, 2026-10-11]
5. **Nicknames are the person's.** A nickname is private to the account
   that wrote it; in-game names are what Elixir says in public.
   [Jamie, 2026-09-06]
6. **The person owns the switches.** Every mail has its own off switch
   and a stop is never undone; the primary player's clan is followed
   for them, never past a slot or in place of a choice they made.
   [Jamie, 2026-10-08]
7. **The policy pages restate the service in plain terms**, change only
   with Jamie's word, and the code is the policy. [Jamie, 2026-09-28]

## What it never does

No ads, brokers, selling or sharing, engagement scoring, automation on
read state, or commercial targeting from game data. Analytics is
client-side Tinylytics only; the servers send it nothing. [Jamie,
2026-09-19, 2026-09-24]

## When a person leaves

Removal is by request. The account and everything that is the
account's goes; the players' game history stays, as any untracked
subject's does. [Jamie, 2026-10-12; see the record]

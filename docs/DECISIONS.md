# Decisions

Where things stand, one line each. The code and the public docs
(`apps/site/src/docs/`) describe behaviour; this file records the choices
behind it. The reasoning behind a line is in git history
(`git log -S "<words from the line>" -- docs/`). A change is a note in
`docs/notes/` plus an edit here, written as the current approach, never
as "was X, now Y".

**This is a working record, not a rulebook.** Elixir is being built, and
its direction moves as we learn. A line is the best current answer as of
its date. Many lines came from a short answer in the middle of other
work, and the date says how fresh that answer is. When a line no longer
fits what is being built, say so and change it: that is the file
working, not a rule being broken.

Every line ends with where it came from:

- **[Jamie, date]** is product direction from Jamie as of that date.
  Build to it. If it looks out of date, or two lines pull against each
  other, go with the newer direction and `VISION.md`, update the line,
  and mention it in the pull request; ask Jamie only when the answer
  changes what gets built next.
- **[engineering, date]** is a default an agent chose. Follow it, and
  change it with evidence (a note and an edit, in a pull request) when
  it gets in the way.
- **[Jamie, date; engineering: …]** is Jamie's direction with agent
  detail; the part after "engineering" may be revisited like any
  engineering line.

A few lines are firm because they protect people or money, and they say
so in their own words: privacy (many players are minors), secrets, what
is sent to whom, and what a person is charged. Those change only with
Jamie.

A line says no more than the answer behind it. A guide, skill, page,
comment or test that needs it names the line's heading rather than
restating it in wider words. Quotes in older lines are kept as the
record of what was said that day; new lines say what we do and why, in
plain words.

## Interface

Verified ownership is rechecked when saving after moderation; client facts, private Clan and People items are excluded. Existing post audiences, blocks, limits and delivery choices apply. Others' currently visible replies after that thread's read cursor are new; a link opens the exact item at the first unread reply. Opening the bell does not advance a thread; actual thread pages advance only through the comments served. [Jamie, 2026-11-22]: threads on your own Timeline items join too: someone else's accepted comment on an item about your Verified player, or on your post, joins you, unread from that comment, and the bell shows it new until the thread is read (`docs/notes/2026-11-22-owner-joins-conversation.md`). Reading, cheering and posting alone do not join, and deleting a contribution does not erase private participation. Source access, blocks and moderation are rechecked now. No new push, email, external comment delivery or public participant list. [Jamie, 2026-11-18, approved recommendation 2; engineering, 2026-11-18, private persistence and page cursor defaults; `docs/notes/2026-11-18-my-conversations.md`]

## Scope and footprint

- **Elixir is the missing social layer of Clash Royale** — "Elixir lets you connect with your friends and play together more in Clash Royale": a timeline of the players you follow, built on a recorder of a player, their friends and their clan, read on the website, delivered in mail, and connected to a person's own agent over MCP, which stays core. For normal players: elite or pro tracking and a deck builder are never built. [Jamie, 2026-09-23, 2026-10-02, 2026-11-11; `VISION.md`]
- **No recommendations from Elixir** — Elixir's own outputs (tools, timeline, mail, Stats and the site) give no deck, upgrade or matchup advice and carry no recommendation scoring, priors or candidate generation, over the whole game or any smaller population (friends, clans). Agents (a person's own, and the family's Discord agent) may coach and recommend from Elixir's facts; the suggestion is theirs, never presented as Elixir's. [Jamie, 2026-10-02, 2026-10-11] These stand under the social layer as what Elixir is not, beside its short never list. [Jamie, 2026-11-11, approving A1 of the DECISIONS sort]
- **No game-wide statistics** — no corpus card or deck performance, meta tools, global comparisons or population rollups. The card catalog and art, canonical deck identity, archetype labels, the factual event calendar and scoped personal and clan history stay. [Jamie, 2026-10-02] These stand under the social layer as what Elixir is not, beside its short never list. [Jamie, 2026-11-11, approving A1 of the DECISIONS sort]
- **No leaderboard capture or autonomous elite recording** — board endpoints are retired at plan, lease, submit and ingest (`RETIRED_RECORDING_ENDPOINTS`); a recorded player's own profile still supplies their rank. [Jamie, 2026-10-02]
- **No named recording Collections** — followed players are organized as primary, alt, friend or watching; no replacement grouping, curated creator catalog, always-on recording or popularity quota exemption. A player's owned-card collection is a game fact and stays. [Jamie, 2026-10-02] "Follow my clan" is the clan follow and a follow link makes friend follows; neither is a named group. [Jamie, 2026-11-11, approving A6]
- **History is kept for what someone follows, and a year after** — a roster poll of a clan nobody follows keeps only the followed players on it (membership, role, roster row, the clan's name) and writes no clan day row; a retained battle keeps every participant. What is recorded goes a year after it happened, followed or not ("Elixir keeps a year; operational logs 28 days"), so no trim waits on a subject's last follower. The maintainer's own `ops` recording reason is retired: only people's following records. [Jamie, 2026-10-06, 2026-10-07, 2026-10-12, 2026-11-13]
- **Elixir keeps a year; operational logs 28 days** — "we have historically not put any kind of data expiration or data lifecycle in place. I think that we should go ahead and assume a 1 year data retention." Of the receipts, the MCP call log and the capture audit: "Operational logs i would keep for 28 days." [Jamie, 2026-11-13; the record brief's banner, 2026-11-14: "Retention yes."]

A daily jobs trim (04:40Z) deletes in bounded batches and leaves the rest to the next day. A battle takes its events, rounds, participants and highlights; decks stay (canonical deck identity); an open membership, an unresolved feedback item and an open claim stay whatever their age. Receipts go at 28 days like the other operational logs: role history, war award evidence, the event calendar's read days and a quiet member's days since a poll read the read facts (0259, kept a year), which ingest writes and `{read_facts_backfill}` filled from the receipts before this trim was switched on, in a deploy of its own after the backfill was done. [engineering, 2026-11-13]
- **Elixir rates no player** — no branded or invented player score or rating (Pilot Score, "Elixir Lift"), residual, similarity, playstyle or adoption-cost number. [Jamie, 2026-09-19, 2026-10-11] These stand under the social layer as what Elixir is not, beside its short never list. [Jamie, 2026-11-11, approving A1 of the DECISIONS sort]

## Data model and semantics

- **Tags are the only game IDs** — no surrogate keys; `claim` is the only join between accounts and game data; a claim unlocks history, it never migrates it. [Jamie, 2026-09-03]
- **Store UTC** — every connection's session zone is UTC; time zones are display only. [Jamie, 2026-09-03, 2026-09-23]
- **The game day is the 10:00Z grid** — daily series and `days: N` resolve on it; the war clock follows the policy grid for every clan, with the observed offset reported raw. [Jamie, 2026-09-07, 2026-09-19]
- **Mode discipline** — modes are different games: a rate with no mode carries its per-mode-group split and a note, and `mode: "event"` pools events with a note. A clanmate battle is `casual` even when tagged; an untagged `unknown` is `casual`, a tagged one is event content. [Jamie, 2026-09-22, 2026-09-25, 2026-09-28]
- **`event_tag` is the discriminator** — a null tag is a permanent format grouped by `type`; a tagged battle belongs to its event; never pool across them, and never invent groupings for `trail`. [Jamie, 2026-09-22]
- **Absence needs proof** — a missing capture, observation or profile clan is unknown, never zero or "no clan"; quiet days count only where complete reads bracket them. [engineering, 2026-10-08]
- **Battle-stub identities are not the recorded population** — headline and population counts (badge populations included) count recorded players: direct, or members of a comprehensively recorded clan. Every retained battle still keeps factual rollups for all participants, so a later-followed opponent's history is consistent. [Jamie, 2026-09-23, 2026-09-25, 2026-10-07]
- **A boat defense is not the member's battle** — it leaves every count of a member's own battles; a boat attack stays. [Jamie, 2026-09-24, 2026-09-25]
- **A duel counts as its rounds** — each round is one game with its own tower-less deck and result; `battles_decks` lists round decks apart. [Jamie, 2026-09-26]
- **War facts are weekly aggregates** — no surface splits a member's week by war day; judgments read the race's weekly `decksUsed` against four a war day up to the finish; the day in progress is the game's own `decksUsedToday`; training days are never attendance. [Jamie, 2026-09-24, 2026-09-25]
